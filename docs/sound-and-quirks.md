# Sound and quirks

**Dark, dense, deliberately unnatural: a 1970s bucket-brigade echo and reverb that glues whatever you feed it.**

## Sound

- **Dark and dense.** Eight BBD chips at a slow clock roll off everything above about 3 kHz (−3 dB near 2.6 kHz at the longest delay), so repeats sit warm and deep behind the dry signal rather than ringing out crisp. Works on synths and drums as well as guitar; the original had a separate synth input.
- **Lofi and gritty.** The line aliases and blurs, and each repeat passes back through the filters and the bucket clip, so trails get darker and dirtier as they decay.
- **Unnatural reverb.** Not a spring or plate: a spread of delay taps mixed through a resistor matrix gives a deep, smeared, distinctly artificial space.
- **Randomly modulated.** The chorus is filtered noise added to the clock, so pitch drifts unpredictably. The drift is a fixed amount in Hz, so it warps further at long delays. With fast Speed and high Duration it edges into flanger territory.
- **On the edge.** Below about 7.4 on Duration repeats die away; loud playing rings on longer than soft. From about 7.7 the loop wakes up from its own hiss and self-oscillates.

## Quirks of the original controls

| Control | Quirk |
| --- | --- |
| Echo buttons | They don't pick the echo you hear: the output is always the longest tap. They pick which taps feed back. E1 = classic repeats, E4 = one long echo then a fast quarter-length flutter |
| Button banks | Radio buttons, but pressing several at the same instant latches them all: the "hold = add" on the face |
| Reverb buttons | Each mixes a different spread of taps; stacking them also stacks feedback, a fast track to self-oscillation |
| Echo / Reverb | A two-pole changeover switch: one bank or the other, never both |
| Speed | The clock control is smoothed (27 ms), so moves bend pitch instead of stepping |
| Volume | The input preamp, before everything: it drives the echo and lifts the dry signal (boost-pedal trick). Bypass passes dry at unity, so a boosted Volume drops in level on bypass |
| Chorus | No rate control: it's noise, band-limited to about 0.5–1.6 Hz |

## Who used it

No documented artists or records. A rare cult studio box, prized by experimental guitarists, synth players and producers, and the predecessor of Dynacord's better-known VRS 23.
